Note: This project is entirely vibecoded.

This is Pixel Cat, a silly AI desktop assistant running on qwen3.5:35b-a3b with a context window of 64k tokens. The Cat can do lots of things, like open and close apps, edit text, make new files, wait for messages (for example, from Teams) and respond to them, and even more. Since the AI is local, you need quite a good PC. For the fastest and smoothest experience, you need about 23.2 GB of VRAM and 32 GB of RAM. I only have about 8, and it runs, however, with stutters, and sometimes it is really slow. With /force, the AI acts like the task manager, and it can close apps by “killing” them.

Don’t have a monster PC? No problem. In the settings you can pick a different AI model, from Tiny (0.8B) all the way up to Huge (122B), so there’s something for every computer. The menu even tells you which one fits your PC best and which ones are too big. You can also switch the model just for one chat with the little button in the chat window, so you can use a quick one for small stuff and the big one when things get serious. The model starts loading in the background as soon as the cat wakes up, and if you open the chat too fast, it just tells you to wait a sec.

Normally the cat asks before it does something on your PC. If you trust it, turn on auto mode and it just does everything without asking. Anything that needs admin rights it will always ask about first though, auto mode or not, because I don’t trust it that much either.

Installing: Just run the installer and wait for it to do the magic for you.

Compatibility: Windows 11

You can also play with the cat and throw around a ball that it will chase, so you can waste your time at work (just kidding ;)). The cat is meant as a useful assistant that can help you do work, not ditch it. You can also feed it, which doesn’t really do anything. And there are several more minigames: Laser pointer, Treat toss, Knock it off!, Mouse hunt, Fishing, Hide and seek and Box fort. Just right-click the cat and look under Minigames. The first time you play one, a little window pops up and explains how it works. If you don’t have any windows open for the cat to jump around on, it just brings its own furniture, which disappears again when the game is over. Oh, and not all games are there from the start, you have to unlock them.

That’s where the Cat Pass comes in. Every game gives you XP, which fills up the pass, and every new stage unlocks something: a new minigame, a new skin (11 of them, from simple recolors to a tabby, a calico and even a galaxy cat) or a hat (15 of them, from a party hat to a royal crown). You’ll find all of it in the new Cosmetics tab in the menu.

And if you don’t like how the menus and the chat look, there’s a Style setting with 10 different looks (Cat Chat, Modern White, Modern Black, Midnight, Retro Terminal and more), plus accent colors, sizes and fonts.
